What these layers mean

Arrows point in the selected field direction and are skipped on estimated planet disks. Their length grows logarithmically with strength. The color bar shows the numeric range used for this view; robust colors clip at the 5th/95th percentiles. The full map now defaults to 3-unit samples; zoom buttons and shift-drag are available, and scrolling does not zoom.

Estimated planets are fitted collision disks in the bot’s belief. Disclosed planets are the game’s oracle map, shown only for diagnosis and never used by belief simulations.

Local residual is observed gravity left over after the fitted planets, interpolated near recorded paths. Field-only source and its residual are alternate gravity explanations; they do not create collision disks. Support is spatially local, not a confidence probability.

Belief combines fitted-planet gravity with the observed local residual. Belief + hypothesis adds a tentative field-only source and its local residual. Belief − actual and Hypothesis − actual are vector differences; they require a disclosed map. X/Y coloring is signed and uses a symmetric log transform if logarithmic scaling is selected.

Raw gravity residuals along paths show reconstructed trajectory curvature minus the fitted planet field at each usable sample. They are not independent data from the path and can include numerical or quantization error; use them to locate patterns, then validate corrections against held-out shots.

Trajectories are colored by player id; click one to inspect its endpoint. The protocol records the sampled path but no termination reason. Purple contact dots are endpoints accepted by the contact estimator, not every physical collision. Missing dots can mean the selected belief snapshot has not caught up; “map updating” flags that it is still working. The timeline shows when each event arrived.

Gravity error colors the magnitude of estimated minus actual acceleration. The RMS and p95 values use the visible grid, including samples inside planet disks; they describe field-vector error, not path error. Uncertainty comes from fitted planet covariance when available and does not include omitted planets or all model error.

Dark cells mean the selected layer has no value there: for example, no fitted covariance for uncertainty, or no trajectory support for residual coverage. They do not mean zero gravity.

A decision shows the latest published map snapshot available at that moment. “Map updating: yes” means newer observations may still be queued or fitting; the snapshot may not include every earlier-looking path yet. Parameter-derived uncertainty does not include missing planets or model error. The field-hypothesis distance proxy is not a probability or standard deviation.

Targeting simulation

Simulation uses the Rust engine model, a stationary snapshot of captured players, and the selected gravity model. The actual-map policy result is a counterfactual from the current code, not the policy version that made the recorded choice.
